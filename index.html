What this is

Two things that normally live in separate apps, on one screen. A Kanban board for the day in front of you, with to do, in progress and done side by side. A week-by-week calendar covering three years at once underneath it, so a deadline in eighteen months is visible rather than imagined.

Task apps are good at today and forget that next year exists. Calendars are good at dates and useless for working out what to do this afternoon. Keeping both means keeping two apps that do not know about each other. This is one page that does both, and the calendar is laid out by week rather than by month because that is how work is actually planned.

Who it is for

People who have to think past this week: planning annual leave around public holidays, tracking a project that runs over several quarters, or simply wanting to see how much of the year is already committed. It suits anyone who works to week numbers, which in practice means project managers, supply chain and logistics planners, finance and reporting teams, and anyone whose employer runs on a reporting calendar rather than on months.

Week numbers, done properly

Everyone agrees a year has weeks. Nobody agrees which one is week 1, and most tools quietly pick one answer and hide the choice. This one offers four numbering rules across all seven week starts, so it can match ISO 8601, a US calendar, or whatever convention your employer's reporting weeks actually use.

That matters more than it sounds. ISO 8601 says week 1 is the first week with four or more days in the new year, usually described as the week containing the first Thursday. Those are the same sentence only when weeks start on Monday, because Thursday is the fourth day of a Monday week. Start your week on Sunday and the fourth day is Wednesday, and the two rules give answers a full week apart. See the current week number and how the systems differ.

Public holidays for 247 countries

Pick your country and its public holidays appear on the calendar, so leave gets planned around them instead of into them. Regional and state holidays are available too, kept separate by default so they do not bury the national ones. Browse public holidays by country.

It works without an account

Open it and start typing. Your board is stored in your own browser, so it works offline and on a plane, and nothing is sent anywhere unless you choose to sign in. Signing in exists only to sync the same board across more than one device. You can export everything to a spreadsheet whenever you like, and delete the lot in one action.
