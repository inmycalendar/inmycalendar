About

A day board that lives next to your year

Most task apps forget the calendar. Most calendars forget the tasks. inmycalendar keeps both in one place: a sticky-note board for what you're doing today, and a week-by-week view of the years around it.

How to use it, in ten seconds

  • Add a task — type into any of the three columns (To do, In progress, Done) and press Enter.
  • Move it along — drag it, or use the arrows on the card. The top of a column is your top priority.
  • Switch the day — use the arrows by the date, or press T for today.
  • See the year — open the Calendar tab. Click any day to jump to it; colour a day to categorise it.
  • Track a date — add a birthday or deadline in the right panel and it counts the days for you.

Everything saves automatically in your browser. There is nothing to set up and no account to create.

The board

Three columns — to do, in progress, done — side by side on every screen size, phones included. Each task is a single line so a full day fits without scrolling. Type into a column to add straight to it. Drag to reorder, or use the arrows: the top of a column is your highest priority, so ranking costs nothing to express.

Switch the scope to week or month to see everything from that stretch of time in one read-only list. Click any line to jump back to the day it belongs to.

The calendar

Years sit side by side, laid out by week rather than by month, so a long stretch of time reads at a glance. The arrows in the calendar's own header move the whole window a year at a time. Colour a day and the whole cell takes that colour.

How weeks are counted

Week 1 is the week containing January 1st. The last week is the week containing December 31st. That means the first and last weeks usually borrow a few days from the year next door — those are greyed rather than hidden, because a date you can't see is a date you'll miss.

Keyboard

  • ← / → — previous or next day
  • T — jump to today  ·  N — new task
  • 1 / 2 / 3 — day, week, month  ·  B / C — board, calendar

Where your data lives

In this browser, and nowhere else. No account, no server, no sync. Use Export notes (JSON) in the settings menu to keep a backup, and Import notes (JSON) to restore it or move it to another machine.
